COMPANION CLASSROOM · PART TWO: WHAT LIES BENEATH THE SURFACE

Chapter 4 Study Room

The Test of Authority

Read · Reflect · Discuss · Apply

Use this companion page after reading Chapter 4. It is designed for personal reflection, church classes, and small groups. Take time to consider each question honestly. In a group setting, allow participants to share only what they feel comfortable sharing, listen respectfully, and keep the discussion grounded in Scripture.

Foundational Scripture References

Acts 5:27 to 29; Romans 13:1 to 7; 1 Peter 5:2 to 3

Read the references in context and consider how they relate to the chapter's central teaching.

Personal Reflection and Application

Take time to examine the way you understand authority. Consider the people who have influenced your spiritual development, the expectations you place upon leaders, and the way you respond when someone in authority disagrees with you. Ask God to reveal whether your understanding has been shaped by Scripture, by personal experience, by fear, or by a desire to maintain approval.

Begin by considering your response to legitimate correction. When someone identifies a mistake, are you willing to listen before defending yourself? Can you acknowledge that another person may see something you have overlooked? If you recognize a pattern of resisting correction, identify one practical way to become more teachable without surrendering your responsibility to examine what you are being asked to do.

Next, consider whether you have placed a person in a position of unquestionable trust. Have you ever defended conduct simply because the person involved was someone you admired? Have you ignored a concern because acknowledging it would be painful? If so, ask yourself what it would mean to value truth more than reputation while still treating everyone involved fairly.

If you have experienced the misuse of authority, consider how that experience affects your present relationships. Are you maintaining boundaries that protect your well being, or has disappointment caused you to distrust every person who offers guidance? You do not need to force yourself to trust someone before trust has been earned. You can move carefully, seek support, and allow consistent character to inform your decisions.

Finally, examine the way you exercise authority over others. Whether you lead in your family, workplace, ministry, classroom, or community, consider how your words and decisions affect those who depend upon you. Do you welcome appropriate questions? Do you accept responsibility when you make a mistake? Do you use your position to serve a legitimate purpose, or do you sometimes rely upon it to protect your pride?

Write down three commitments:

  1. One attitude toward authority that I need God to correct.
  2. One boundary or responsibility that I need to address with wisdom and integrity.
  3. One way I can exercise greater humility and accountability in my own relationships.

Pray over these commitments and return to them during the coming week. Remember that discernment begins with a willingness to seek truth, including the truth about ourselves. The purpose is not to become suspicious of everyone, but to become faithful to God in every relationship where authority and responsibility are involved.

Study Questions

  1. Why is a person’s position or title insufficient to establish the character and righteousness of that person?
  2. How can a believer distinguish between prideful resistance to legitimate authority and discernment concerning an improper demand?
  3. What does Matthew 20:25 to 28 teach about the purpose and character of godly leadership?
  4. What are some warning signs that spiritual language is being used to control people rather than guide them toward truth?
  5. Why must leaders remain accountable, and what is the difference between genuine accountability and an attempt to damage someone’s reputation?
  6. How can believers remain teachable without becoming dependent upon another person’s approval for every spiritual decision?
  7. What practical steps can someone take after discovering that a trusted leader has acted wrongly, while avoiding both denial and bitterness?
  8. How can you apply the principles of authority, humility, discernment, and accountability in your own life this week?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the highest authority in my life, and I desire to understand every other responsibility in the light of Your truth. Examine my heart and reveal where I have confused a person’s position with righteousness, where fear has prevented me from asking necessary questions, and where pride has caused me to resist correction that I needed to receive.

Teach me to honor those who lead faithfully without placing any human being beyond accountability. Give me discernment to recognize the difference between godly guidance and manipulation, between legitimate correction and intimidation, and between humility and the surrender of my conscience to an improper demand. Keep me from becoming rebellious when I need to be teachable, and keep me from becoming silent when truth requires a responsible response.

Help those who exercise authority to understand the seriousness of their responsibility. Give them wisdom, integrity, compassion, and the humility to acknowledge their mistakes. Protect those who are vulnerable to manipulation or abuse, and provide them with trustworthy support and appropriate help. Where wrongdoing has occurred, let truth be established and let accountability be pursued with fairness and courage.

Search my own life as well. Show me where I have misused influence, dismissed another person’s concerns, or expected others to accept what I would not be willing to examine myself. Teach me to lead through service, to correct with wisdom, and to receive correction without allowing pride to govern my response.

When I have been disappointed by people I trusted, help me remain grounded in You. Keep bitterness from becoming my identity and fear from controlling every future relationship. Give me the wisdom to establish necessary boundaries, the courage to seek help when it is needed, and the grace to recognize integrity when it is demonstrated.

May my loyalty to You never become an excuse for mistreating others, and may my respect for people never become an excuse for abandoning truth. Let my life reflect the character of Jesus Christ in the way I follow, the way I lead, and the way I respond when authority is tested. In the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Affirmation of Faith

Jesus Christ is the highest authority in my life. I will honor legitimate leadership while remembering that every person remains accountable to God. I will receive correction with humility, examine spiritual claims with discernment, and refuse to confuse a human preference with the truth of God’s Word. I will not use questions as a weapon, nor will I surrender my conscience to fear or manipulation. I will seek truth with integrity, maintain appropriate boundaries, and take responsibility for my own decisions. Whether I am leading or following, I will strive to act with humility, courage, fairness, and love. My faith is anchored in Jesus Christ, and my desire is to live in a way that honors His authority above all others.

Looking Ahead

Understanding the test of authority helps us recognize that leadership carries both influence and responsibility. Yet recognizing the limits of human authority is only part of the journey. We must also examine the character of those who have been entrusted with the responsibility to lead, teach, guide, and care for others.

In Chapter 5, The Mantle and the Master, we will explore the difference between carrying a position and fulfilling its purpose. We will consider the responsibility that accompanies spiritual leadership, the danger of confusing influence with ownership, and the importance of remembering that every calling must remain submitted to Jesus Christ. The central question will be whether those entrusted with authority are using their influence to serve the purposes of God or to build something around themselves.
