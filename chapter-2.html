Submission Begins Within

Proverbs 3:5

Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.

Submission is one of the most frequently discussed subjects in Christian teaching, yet it is also one of the most easily misunderstood. Some people associate submission exclusively with following instructions, respecting leadership, or yielding when another person makes a decision. Others approach the subject with apprehension because they have witnessed submission being demanded in ways that produced fear, confusion, or unnecessary control. Still others resist the very idea of submission because they associate it with weakness, the loss of personal freedom, or the inability to make decisions for themselves. These different responses reveal why the subject must be examined carefully through the Word of God rather than defined entirely by personal experiences or human expectations.

Biblical submission begins with our relationship with God. Before we can understand what it means to respond appropriately to the authority entrusted to another person, we must first understand what it means to surrender our own hearts to the Lord. Submission is not merely an outward response to an instruction, nor is it simply the willingness to cooperate when doing so benefits us. It involves the deeper recognition that God’s wisdom is greater than our own and that His truth must govern our decisions, even when our preferences point in another direction.

This is where the real examination begins, because the heart often reveals its resistance long before that resistance becomes visible through outward behavior. We may agree with God’s Word when it confirms what we already believe, but struggle when it challenges an attitude we have defended for years. We may welcome instruction when it brings us comfort, yet become defensive when it exposes something that requires change. We may pray for God’s direction while quietly hoping that He will approve the outcome we have already chosen. In such moments, the question is not whether we believe that God has authority. The question is whether we are willing to surrender our own judgment when His Word reveals that we need to change.

Proverbs 3:5–6 directs our attention to the importance of trusting the Lord rather than relying exclusively upon our own understanding. This principle does not require us to abandon sound judgment or stop asking questions. Instead, it reminds us that our understanding is limited and that we need God’s wisdom to direct our lives. Genuine submission requires the humility to acknowledge that we do not always see the full picture, recognize every consequence, or understand why a particular response is necessary. We must learn to seek God’s direction sincerely rather than treating prayer as a way to obtain confirmation for decisions we have already made.

A surrendered heart remains willing to be taught. It recognizes that growth sometimes requires us to reconsider what we believe, acknowledge where our understanding has been incomplete, and release attitudes that have become obstacles to spiritual maturity. This process may be uncomfortable, especially when correction touches an area in which we feel confident or when obedience requires us to admit that we were wrong. Nevertheless, the willingness to be corrected is essential to spiritual growth because a heart that refuses to learn will eventually become governed by its own assumptions.

Submission, therefore, must begin with an honest recognition of who God is and who we are before Him. We are not called to make ourselves the final authority over truth. We are called to seek the Lord, receive His Word, and allow His wisdom to direct our lives. The more clearly we understand His authority, the more readily we can recognize that surrender is not the loss of something good, but the willingness to trust the One whose wisdom exceeds our own.

The Difference Between Outward Compliance and Inner Surrender

One of the most important distinctions in understanding submission is the difference between doing what is expected and possessing a heart that is genuinely willing to follow God. Outward compliance can sometimes conceal inward resistance. A person may follow an instruction while harboring resentment, obey a decision while planning to undermine it, or maintain an appearance of agreement while refusing to accept the truth behind what has been communicated. Although outward actions matter, they do not always reveal the motives that produced them.

This does not mean that every person who struggles emotionally has a rebellious heart. People may need time to process difficult circumstances, work through disappointment, or seek clarity before they can respond with peace. Honest questions and sincere struggles are not automatically evidence of disobedience. The issue is whether we remain willing to pursue truth and respond faithfully to God, or whether we allow our preferences to become an excuse for rejecting what we know to be right.

Consider the difference between a person who obeys because they genuinely believe an instruction is right and a person who complies only because they fear the consequences of refusing. Both may perform the same outward action, yet their motives and understanding differ significantly. Fear may produce temporary compliance, but it does not necessarily produce spiritual maturity. Genuine submission develops through trust in God, an understanding of His Word, and a willingness to follow Him even when obedience requires sacrifice.

Romans 12:1–2 calls believers to present themselves to God and to experience the renewing of their minds. This passage reminds us that the Christian life involves more than changing outward behavior to satisfy expectations. God desires transformation that reaches into our thinking, our values, and the motives behind our decisions. As our minds are renewed by His truth, we become better equipped to recognize what pleases Him and to respond with greater wisdom.

1 Peter 5:2–3

Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind; Neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being ensamples to the flock.

Matthew 26:39

And he went a little further, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.

Romans 12:1–2

I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service. And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.

This renewal takes place through an ongoing relationship with God. We learn to examine our thoughts in light of Scripture, recognize when our feelings are influencing our conclusions, and distinguish between what we want and what is right. We begin to understand that a surrendered heart does not mean we will never experience conflict within ourselves. It means that when conflict arises, we remain willing to bring our desires before God and allow His truth to guide our response.

The heart of submission is revealed most clearly when obedience is not convenient. It is relatively easy to agree with an instruction that supports our plans, confirms our opinions, or brings us a desired outcome. The deeper test comes when obedience requires patience, humility, forgiveness, or the willingness to release control. In those moments, we discover whether our commitment to God is rooted in trust or dependent upon circumstances remaining favorable.

When the Will of God Conflicts With Our Own

There are times when following God requires us to confront the difference between what we desire and what His Word requires. These moments can reveal how strongly we have attached ourselves to particular outcomes and how difficult it can be to surrender our expectations. We may know what Scripture teaches while hoping that our circumstances will somehow justify an exception. We may understand the importance of forgiveness while holding tightly to the right to remain resentful. We may recognize the need for humility while struggling to admit that another person has identified a genuine weakness in our conduct.

The struggle between our preferences and the will of God is not new. Scripture repeatedly reveals that faithful obedience can require difficult choices, and one of the clearest examples appears in the life of Jesus Christ. In Matthew 26:39, during His prayer in Gethsemane, Jesus expressed the anguish associated with the suffering that lay before Him while submitting Himself to the will of the Father. His prayer reveals the seriousness of the moment and the reality of the suffering He faced. His obedience was not a casual decision made without understanding the cost. It was a faithful surrender in the face of profound anguish.

We must approach this example with reverence because the suffering and obedience of Jesus occupy a unique place in the work of redemption. His experience nevertheless directs our attention to the seriousness of surrendering our will to God. Faithfulness is not measured by how easy a decision feels or by whether we experience immediate relief after making it. Sometimes obedience requires us to move forward without having every question answered, trusting God’s wisdom while remaining faithful to what He has revealed.

For believers, this may involve releasing a personal ambition that conflicts with biblical integrity, accepting responsibility for a mistake, choosing forgiveness instead of retaliation, or remaining faithful to a responsibility when circumstances become difficult. It may also require us to reconsider a decision after discovering that our motives were influenced by pride or fear. These situations are different in their details, but they share a common demand: we must be willing to let God’s truth take precedence over our desire to control the outcome.

It is important to recognize that surrendering our will to God does not mean assuming that every difficult circumstance has been directly arranged by Him or that every painful experience must be accepted without question. Scripture calls us to trust God while also exercising wisdom, seeking help when necessary, confronting wrongdoing appropriately, and making responsible decisions. Submission is not an excuse for passivity in the face of harm, nor does it require us to pretend that injustice is acceptable. It means that even when circumstances are painful or another person has acted wrongly, we remain committed to responding according to God’s truth.

The question we must ask is whether our desire for a particular outcome has become so powerful that we are unwilling to consider what God requires of us. We may pray for direction while ignoring the principles He has already established in Scripture. We may ask Him to change another person’s behavior while refusing to examine our own. We may seek peace without accepting the responsibility to make things right where we have caused harm. A surrendered heart is willing to bring these contradictions into the light and allow God to correct them.

Submission Is Not the Worship of Human Authority

Acts 5:29

Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.

Because this book examines the subject of authority, we must establish an important boundary. Submission to God must never be confused with placing a human being in the position that belongs to Him alone. No pastor, teacher, ministry leader, employer, parent, or other person possesses unlimited authority over another human being. Every legitimate position of authority carries responsibilities and limits, and every person remains accountable to God for how that authority is exercised.

Some people have been taught to associate submission with silence, even when something is clearly wrong. Others have experienced situations in which religious language was used to discourage questions, prevent accountability, or make people feel that disagreeing with a leader was equivalent to disobeying God. Such practices must not be accepted merely because they are presented in spiritual terms. A person’s title does not make every decision righteous, and a leader’s confidence does not establish that every instruction reflects the will of God.

Acts 5:29 demonstrates that the apostles recognized a responsibility to obey God rather than comply with a human demand that conflicted with His command. Their response reminds us that our ultimate allegiance belongs to God. When a human instruction requires us to violate Scripture, participate in wrongdoing, conceal abuse, or act against the clear requirements of righteousness, we must not assume that submission requires compliance. Such circumstances call for prayerful discernment, courage, and a commitment to follow God.

At the same time, we must not use this principle to justify resistance whenever an instruction challenges our preferences. A person may disagree with a leader’s approach without having a biblical reason to reject the instruction itself. A decision may be inconvenient without being sinful, and correction may feel uncomfortable without being unjust. We must learn to distinguish personal disappointment from genuine wrongdoing and examine our motives before concluding that our resistance is necessary.

This requires maturity because it is often easier to defend our own position than to examine it honestly. We may be tempted to label an instruction controlling when it simply asks us to accept responsibility, or to accuse someone of disrespect when they are raising a legitimate concern. Conversely, we may be tempted to excuse manipulation because the person involved holds a respected position. Neither response reflects sound discernment. We must evaluate conduct according to Scripture, recognize the limits of human authority, and remain willing to acknowledge when our own understanding needs correction.

True submission to God produces both humility and discernment. Humility keeps us from assuming that our personal opinions are always correct, while discernment keeps us from treating every human demand as though it carries divine authority. These qualities must work together if we are to walk faithfully in the truth.

The Responsibility of the Leader

The heart of submission cannot be discussed honestly without considering the heart of the person who leads. Authority creates responsibility, and those entrusted with influence must recognize that their conduct affects the people they serve. A leader may possess knowledge, experience, and a genuine desire to help others, yet still need to examine the motives that influence their decisions. The responsibility of leadership is not fulfilled merely by giving instructions or expecting others to comply. It requires a commitment to serve faithfully, communicate truthfully, and exercise influence according to the character of Jesus Christ.

1 Peter 5:2–3 provides important instruction concerning the responsibility of spiritual shepherds. The passage challenges leaders to care for those entrusted to them without allowing selfish ambition or a desire for domination to shape their conduct. This principle should influence the way pastors and ministry leaders understand their roles. The people in their care are not possessions to be controlled, and their questions are not automatically threats to the leader’s authority. They are individuals who deserve faithful instruction, genuine care, and leadership that reflects the principles of Scripture.

A leader who understands this responsibility will recognize that accountability is not an enemy of spiritual authority. It is part of exercising authority faithfully. Leaders must remain willing to examine their decisions, acknowledge errors, and receive appropriate correction when their conduct falls short of the standard they teach. A position of influence increases the importance of integrity because the consequences of a leader’s actions can extend far beyond the leader personally.

The same principle applies to every person who has responsibility for others. Parents must recognize that guiding their children requires more than demanding obedience. Employers must understand that authority over a workplace does not justify mistreatment. Teachers must recognize that responsibility for a learning environment must be exercised with fairness and care. Pastors must remember that spiritual leadership exists to serve God’s purposes rather than personal pride. In every setting, authority should be exercised with an understanding that people deserve dignity and that those who lead remain accountable for their choices.

The leader must also recognize that submission to God includes a willingness to be corrected by the truth. It is inconsistent to teach humility while refusing to admit mistakes, or to demand accountability from others while avoiding it personally. Such inconsistency damages trust and weakens the integrity of leadership. Genuine spiritual maturity requires leaders to model the principles they teach, not because they will never fail, but because they remain willing to acknowledge failure and pursue what is right.

When leaders exercise authority faithfully, they create conditions in which people can grow, learn, ask sincere questions, and develop greater maturity. When authority is exercised through fear or manipulation, people may comply outwardly while becoming increasingly afraid to speak honestly. The difference matters because God is not glorified when people are controlled through intimidation. His truth must be handled faithfully, and His people must be served with the humility and integrity that reflect the example of Jesus Christ.

The Danger of Pride and Resistance

One of the greatest obstacles to submission is pride, because pride makes it difficult for us to recognize the limits of our own understanding. It encourages us to protect our reputation, defend our decisions, and interpret correction as a threat rather than an opportunity to grow. When pride governs the heart, even sound instruction can become difficult to receive because the person listening is more concerned with maintaining the appearance of being right than with discovering what is true.

James 4:6–10 emphasizes the relationship between humility and our response to God. These verses direct us toward a posture of dependence upon Him rather than self exaltation. Humility does not mean denying the gifts God has given us, pretending that we have no value, or allowing others to treat us without dignity. It means recognizing that our abilities, understanding, and opportunities do not make us independent of God. We remain dependent upon His wisdom and responsible for how we use what He has entrusted to us.

Pride can appear in different forms. It may be expressed through open defiance, but it can also appear through quiet resentment, constant defensiveness, or the refusal to acknowledge that another person has made a valid point. It can lead a leader to dismiss legitimate concerns and lead a believer to reject correction simply because it came from someone they dislike. It can even hide behind religious language when a person uses spiritual convictions to avoid examining personal motives.

This is why we must be careful when evaluating our own responses. It is easy to identify pride when another person behaves arrogantly, but more difficult to recognize it when it appears within our own reasoning. We may tell ourselves that we are defending truth when we are actually protecting our reputation. We may insist that we are standing on principle when our reaction is being driven by wounded pride. We may claim that we are refusing manipulation when we are simply unwilling to accept any instruction that conflicts with our preferences.

Honest examination requires us to ask whether our response is consistent with Scripture and whether we are willing to consider evidence that challenges our conclusion. It requires us to distinguish between a legitimate concern and a defensive reaction, between a firm conviction and an unwillingness to learn. This process does not guarantee that we will always agree with others, but it helps us respond with greater maturity and less dependence upon pride.

A humble heart can stand firmly for truth while remaining willing to listen. It can disagree without becoming cruel, receive correction without collapsing into shame, and acknowledge a mistake without believing that doing so destroys personal worth. Such humility creates room for growth because it allows truth to take priority over the need to preserve an image.

Learning to Surrender Daily

Submission is not a single decision that removes every future struggle. It is an ongoing commitment to bring our hearts and conduct before God and remain willing to follow His truth. Each day presents opportunities to practice humility, exercise discernment, receive correction, and make choices that reflect our commitment to Jesus Christ. Some of these opportunities may involve significant decisions, while others may appear ordinary, such as choosing our words carefully during a disagreement or admitting that we misunderstood someone.

The ordinary moments matter because our character is formed through the repeated choices we make. A person who consistently refuses to acknowledge small mistakes may find it increasingly difficult to accept correction when the consequences become serious. A leader who repeatedly dismisses questions may gradually create an environment in which people are afraid to speak honestly. A believer who continually allows resentment to shape their responses may become less willing to pursue reconciliation or consider another person’s perspective. The habits we cultivate influence the kind of people we become.

Learning to surrender daily begins with a willingness to listen to the Word of God and examine our responses in its light. When we recognize that we have acted wrongly, we must be willing to repent rather than search for excuses. When we discover that our understanding is incomplete, we must remain teachable rather than treating new information as a threat. When we face a difficult decision, we must seek wisdom and consider our responsibilities rather than allowing immediate feelings to determine our actions.

Prayer also plays an important role in this process, not because it removes our responsibility to act wisely, but because it directs our attention toward God and reminds us of our dependence upon Him. We can ask Him to reveal the motives behind our decisions, give us courage to obey His Word, and help us respond with humility when our pride is challenged. We can ask for wisdom to recognize when an instruction is legitimate and when a boundary must be maintained. We can also ask for the strength to address wrongdoing honestly without allowing anger or bitterness to govern our conduct.

1 Peter 5:5–7 connects humility with our relationship to God and our response to the concerns we carry. This teaching reminds us that surrender does not require us to pretend that we have no struggles. We can acknowledge our concerns honestly while entrusting ourselves to God and remaining responsible for the choices before us. We do not need to control every person or circumstance in order to remain faithful. We must learn to do what is right, seek the wisdom we need, and trust God with what lies beyond our control.

Over time, this daily surrender develops a more stable foundation for spiritual maturity. We become less dependent upon always having our own way and more willing to pursue what is right. We learn that correction can help us grow, that disagreement does not always require conflict, and that humility does not weaken conviction. We begin to recognize that submission is not a passive surrender of responsibility, but an active commitment to let God’s truth guide the way we live.

A Heart That Belongs to God

The heart of submission is ultimately revealed through our willingness to belong to God without reserving areas of our lives where His truth is permitted to speak only when we agree with it. It means recognizing that our preferences matter, but they do not become the final authority over righteousness. Our feelings deserve honest attention, but they cannot always determine the right course of action. Our experiences influence how we understand the world, but they must not prevent us from examining what Scripture teaches.

A surrendered heart does not become perfect overnight, and it does not mean that every difficult feeling disappears. Believers can experience uncertainty, disappointment, and internal struggle while continuing to seek God faithfully. What distinguishes a surrendered heart is its willingness to remain open to the truth, repent when necessary, and pursue obedience even when doing so requires sacrifice. The goal is not to eliminate every question or become incapable of disagreement. The goal is to develop the wisdom and humility necessary to respond faithfully to God.

For the believer, this means learning to follow Jesus Christ with sincerity rather than treating obedience as a performance designed to gain approval from others. For the leader, it means recognizing that authority must remain under the direction of God’s Word and that responsibility requires humility, service, and accountability. For the Church, it means cultivating a culture in which truth can be examined honestly, correction can produce growth, and no person is elevated beyond the standard of Scripture.

The journey beyond the veil requires us to look beyond outward appearances and examine the motives that shape our responses. It asks us to consider whether we are willing to surrender our pride, release our need for control, and trust God when His truth challenges our preferences. It also asks leaders to consider whether their own hearts remain surrendered to the One whose authority they claim to represent.

The heart of submission is not found in the unquestioning elevation of another human being. It is found in a life that recognizes the authority of God and responds to Him with humility, trust, and obedience. When this foundation is established, we become better equipped to understand the responsibilities of human relationships without confusing them with our ultimate responsibility before the Lord.

The question is no longer simply whether we are willing to follow instructions. It is whether we are willing to allow God to shape the heart from which our decisions, words, and actions flow. That is where genuine submission begins, and it is the foundation upon which every other discussion of authority must stand.
