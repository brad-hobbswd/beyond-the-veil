BEYOND THE VEIL

Part Four: Living in the Light

Revealing the Truth About Authority, Obedience, and the Heart

By Bradley Hobbs

There comes a point in every believer’s walk when understanding truth must become more than an intellectual exercise. We can study authority, recognize the dangers of spiritual manipulation, identify the importance of accountability, and explain the difference between humility and control. Yet the real test comes when that truth reaches our own hearts and requires us to change the way we think, respond, lead, follow, forgive, and live.

Living in the light requires an honest relationship with God. It means allowing His truth to expose what we would rather keep hidden, correcting the attitudes we have learned to defend, and surrendering the desire to appear right when we need to become right. It also means recognizing that spiritual maturity is not measured by how effectively we identify the failures of others, but by our willingness to let God examine our own lives.

One of the greatest dangers in a discussion about authority is becoming so focused on what others should do that we neglect what God is asking of us. We may recognize pride in a leader while overlooking pride in our own responses. We may demand that others accept correction while making excuses when correction reaches us. We may speak passionately about truth while refusing to acknowledge the places where our own conduct falls short of it.

The light of God’s truth does not expose our hearts merely to leave us condemned. It brings what is hidden into the open so that we can respond with repentance, humility, and a renewed commitment to righteousness. When we recognize that our attitudes or actions have been wrong, we have an opportunity to turn away from them rather than continue defending what needs to change.

This does not mean accepting every accusation or assuming that every painful experience is our fault. Honest self examination requires discernment. We must take responsibility for our own choices without accepting responsibility for another person’s misconduct. We must remain open to correction without allowing shame, intimidation, or manipulation to determine what we believe about ourselves. Living in the light means being willing to acknowledge what is true, even when that truth requires us to reconsider our assumptions.

It also means refusing to remain bound to the failures of others. We cannot control whether every leader will repent, whether every relationship will be restored, or whether every community will respond appropriately when concerns are raised. We can, however, choose how we respond before God. We can pursue healing without denying what happened, establish boundaries without surrendering to bitterness, and continue growing in faith without allowing another person’s actions to become the final authority over our lives.

In this final part of our journey, we will examine the heart that remains teachable and the life that is continually brought under the authority of Jesus Christ. We will consider the importance of repentance, the courage required to receive correction, and the responsibility to keep our own lives aligned with the truth we profess. The goal is not simply to recognize what is wrong in the world around us. The goal is to become people who are willing to walk in the light of God’s truth.

CHAPTER 7

The Heart That Can Be Corrected

Spiritual maturity is revealed not by the absence of mistakes, but by the willingness to acknowledge them and respond to correction. A heart that can be corrected does not treat every challenge as an attack or every admission of error as a loss of dignity. It recognizes that growth requires humility, honesty, and a continuing willingness to submit personal attitudes and decisions to the truth of God’s Word.

This chapter will examine the difference between conviction and condemnation, the danger of defending pride, and the importance of genuine repentance. It will challenge us to consider whether we are as willing to receive correction as we are to offer it to others. Most importantly, it will call us to understand that the same God who exposes what is wrong also calls us toward transformation.

CHAPTER 8

When Everything Answers to God

Every position, responsibility, relationship, and decision must ultimately be understood beneath the authority of God. Human authority has boundaries, spiritual leadership carries accountability, and every believer remains responsible for how life is lived. Nothing about a person’s title, influence, history, or reputation removes the need to answer to the truth.

This final chapter will bring the central themes of the book together by examining what it means to live with an eternal perspective and a surrendered heart. We will consider how to honor legitimate authority without worshiping human influence, how to remain humble without becoming vulnerable to manipulation, and how to pursue righteousness without allowing bitterness to take control. The goal is a life in which our beliefs and actions agree, our convictions remain grounded in Scripture, and our ultimate allegiance belongs to Jesus Christ.

Chapter 6: When Authority Goes Wrong

When leadership loses sight of its responsibility before God, influence can become control, correction can become intimidation, and spiritual guidance can become a means of protecting human interests. Recognizing these dangers requires discernment, courage, and a commitment to truth. We must learn how to respond when authority is misused without abandoning humility, excusing wrongdoing, or allowing the failures of people to determine our relationship with Jesus Christ.
