CHAPTER STUDY GUIDE · PART TWO: WHAT LIES BENEATH THE SURFACE

Chapter 3 Study Guide

When Obedience Costs You

Read · Reflect · Discuss · Apply

Use this companion page after reading Chapter 3. It is designed for personal reflection, church classes, and small groups. Take time to consider each question honestly. In a group setting, allow participants to share only what they feel comfortable sharing, listen respectfully, and keep the discussion grounded in Scripture.

Foundational Scripture References

Genesis 22:1 to 14; 1 Samuel 15:22 to 23; Matthew 26:39; Acts 5:29; James 1:2 to 4

Read the references in context and consider how they relate to the chapter's central teaching.

Personal Reflection and Application

Take time to consider the areas of your life where obedience has become difficult. Do not begin by examining what other people should change. Begin by asking God to reveal the condition of your own heart. The purpose of reflection is not to produce shame or to convince yourself that every difficult circumstance is your fault. It is to identify the decisions, attitudes, and responsibilities that genuinely belong to you.

Consider whether there is an instruction in God’s Word that you understand but have delayed putting into practice. Perhaps you need to acknowledge a mistake, make restitution, release resentment, speak honestly, or stop participating in a behavior you know is wrong. If the next step is clear, identify one practical action you can take. Do not allow the desire for a perfect plan to become another reason for postponing obedience.

Examine your response to correction. When someone points out a mistake, do you listen carefully, or do you immediately prepare a defense? Can you distinguish between correction that is justified and accusations that are unfair? Ask God to help you remain humble enough to receive truth while maintaining the discernment necessary to recognize manipulation or unreasonable demands.

Reflect on the role approval plays in your decisions. Have you remained silent because you feared disappointing someone? Have you agreed to something that troubled your conscience because you wanted to preserve a relationship or position? If so, consider how you can respond with integrity and wisdom. You do not need to become confrontational to become courageous. You need to become committed to truth.

Finally, examine whether you have confused suffering with faithfulness. If you are experiencing conflict, consider your own conduct honestly while also examining whether the expectations placed upon you are appropriate. Seek wise, trustworthy counsel when the situation is complicated, and prioritize safety if there is a risk of harm. You are responsible for your choices, but you are not responsible for making another person behave righteously.

Write down the following three commitments in your own words:

  1. One area in which I need to obey God’s Word without further delay.
  2. One fear or desire for approval that has been influencing my decisions.
  3. One wise and practical step I can take to respond with greater faithfulness, humility, and discernment.

Return to these commitments in prayer throughout the coming week. Allow your progress to be measured not by how impressive your intentions sound, but by whether your decisions increasingly reflect the truth you profess to believe.

Study Questions

  1. Why is there a difference between believing in obedience and practicing obedience when it becomes costly?
  2. What does Abraham’s experience in Genesis 22:1 to 14 teach about surrendering our expectations to God, and why must this account never be used to justify harmful demands from human leaders?
  3. According to First Samuel 15:22 to 23, how can partial obedience become a way of preserving personal control while appearing spiritually faithful?
  4. How does the account of Jesus in Gethsemane help us understand the relationship between emotional struggle and genuine surrender?
  5. What is the difference between resisting legitimate correction out of pride and refusing a demand that violates God’s truth?
  6. How can the desire for approval cause a believer to compromise integrity, and what practical steps can help correct this pattern?
  7. Why is it important to recognize that suffering does not automatically prove that a person is obeying God?
  8. What specific area of your life requires a more faithful response to God’s Word, and what action will you take to address it?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, I come before You acknowledging that obedience is sometimes easier to profess than to practice. I recognize that there are moments when my preferences, fears, expectations, and desire for approval compete with the truth You have placed before me. Search my heart and reveal anything within me that resists Your correction or attempts to preserve control where surrender is required.

Give me the courage to obey Your Word even when obedience requires sacrifice. Teach me to receive correction without allowing pride to govern my response, and give me the humility to acknowledge when I have been wrong. Help me recognize the difference between a difficult but necessary act of obedience and a demand that contradicts Your truth. Keep me from confusing human pressure with Your authority, and do not allow fear to silence my conscience when righteousness requires me to act.

Strengthen me when I face disappointment, misunderstanding, or the loss of approval. Help me remain faithful without becoming bitter, courageous without becoming arrogant, and discerning without becoming suspicious of everyone around me. Where I have caused harm, lead me toward repentance and restoration. Where I have been treated unjustly, guide me toward truth, wise counsel, and the steps necessary to protect what is right.

Let my obedience arise from a heart that trusts You rather than from a desire to earn recognition or control an outcome. Teach me to surrender what You ask me to surrender, to accept responsibility for what belongs to me, and to stand firm when faithfulness requires a clear and honest response. May my life demonstrate that Your truth is not merely something I speak about, but the foundation upon which I make my decisions.

I place my will, my ambitions, my relationships, and my future before You. Continue shaping my heart until my desire to please You becomes greater than my desire to protect my comfort. Let every decision bring me closer to the person You are calling me to become. In the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Affirmation of Faith

I will not measure obedience by how comfortable it feels or how much approval it brings. I will seek the truth of God’s Word and take responsibility for the decisions entrusted to me. I will remain humble enough to receive correction, courageous enough to stand for what is right, and discerning enough to recognize the limits of human authority. I will not use suffering to prove my spirituality, nor will I use discomfort as an excuse to abandon what God has made clear. My faith belongs to Jesus Christ, and my desire is to live with a heart that remains surrendered, teachable, and faithful. Even when obedience carries a cost, I will seek to honor God with integrity, wisdom, and love.

Looking Ahead

Obedience reveals the condition of our hearts, but it also raises an important question: how do we know whether the authority we are following is being exercised according to God’s truth? It is possible to resist legitimate authority because of pride, but it is also possible to comply with human demands that exceed their rightful limits. Spiritual maturity requires us to recognize the difference.

In Chapter 4, The Test of Authority, we will examine how authority must be evaluated, why position alone does not establish righteousness, and how believers can remain respectful without surrendering discernment. We will consider the responsibility of those who lead and the responsibility of those who follow, remembering that both stand accountable before God. The goal is not suspicion or rebellion, but a faith that honors legitimate authority while remaining firmly anchored in the truth of Jesus Christ.
