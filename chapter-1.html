PART ONE · DISCOVERING GOD’S AUTHORITY

Chapter 1

The Authority Behind Everything

Bradley Hobbs

Foundational Scripture References

  • Psalm 115:3
  • Isaiah 46:9 to 10
  • Matthew 28:18
  • John 14:15
  • Acts 5:29
  • Romans 13:1 to 7
  • Hebrews 4:12 to 13

Understanding the Foundation

Before we can understand the true meaning of submission, obedience, and spiritual authority, we must first establish a foundation that cannot be moved by human opinion, personal experience, or the influence of another person. That foundation is the authority of God Himself. Everything we believe about spiritual leadership, our responsibility as believers, and the way we respond when our faith is tested must begin with an understanding of who God is and what His authority means for our lives. If we fail to recognize God as the highest authority, we will eventually struggle to understand the boundaries of human authority, the importance of obedience, and the responsibility that accompanies every position of influence.

One of the greatest dangers in the Christian life is becoming familiar with the things of God without allowing our understanding of Him to shape the way we live. We can attend church faithfully, listen to preaching, study the Scriptures, and speak confidently about spiritual matters while still making decisions according to our own desires. We may acknowledge that God is sovereign while becoming frustrated whenever His direction differs from our expectations. We may declare that Jesus Christ is Lord while quietly reserving certain areas of our lives where we expect to remain in control. The question is not whether we know the right words to describe God’s authority, but whether we are willing to recognize that His authority applies to every part of our lives.

God does not become sovereign because we acknowledge Him, and His authority does not depend upon whether we agree with His decisions. Psalm 115:3 establishes that God is in the heavens and acts according to His will. This truth reminds us that His authority is not granted by human beings, nor is it dependent upon the approval of those He has created. He does not need our permission to be God, and we do not possess the ability to make His truth less authoritative by refusing to accept it. Our responsibility is to understand who He is, learn what He has revealed through His Word, and bring our lives into alignment with His will.

This is where genuine spiritual maturity begins. It begins when we stop treating obedience as something that is required only when we understand every circumstance or agree with every instruction. It begins when we recognize that God’s wisdom exceeds our own and that His perspective is not limited by the fears, preferences, and incomplete understanding that often influence our decisions. Isaiah 46:9 to 10 directs our attention to the uniqueness of God and His ability to declare His purposes and accomplish His will. When we consider the greatness of the One we serve, we begin to understand why trust and obedience cannot depend entirely upon our ability to see what lies ahead.

There will be moments when God’s Word confronts something we have believed for years, and there will be circumstances in which following His principles requires us to release an outcome we desperately wanted to control. We may discover that an attitude we considered justified has become a source of bitterness, or that a decision we defended confidently was influenced more by pride than by wisdom. These moments are not opportunities to protect our image by resisting the truth. They are opportunities to grow in humility and allow God to correct our understanding. The person who truly recognizes God’s authority must remain willing to be taught, because a heart that refuses correction cannot fully benefit from the truth it claims to honor.

The Authority of Jesus Christ

The authority of God is not an abstract principle that exists separately from our relationship with Jesus Christ. The lordship of Jesus is central to the Christian life, and Matthew 28:18 records His declaration concerning the authority given to Him in heaven and on earth. His authority reaches beyond the walls of a church building, beyond the boundaries of a ministry, and beyond the moments when we gather with other believers. He is Lord over our conduct, our relationships, our private decisions, our use of influence, and the motives that shape the choices we make when no one else is watching.

It is possible to confess Jesus as Lord while living as though His instructions are optional. We may celebrate His power, speak about His goodness, and seek His help when we face difficulties, yet resist the changes He calls us to make. We may want His promises without welcoming His correction, desire His direction without surrendering our preferences, and seek His blessings while avoiding the responsibilities that accompany a life of discipleship. Such a divided approach reveals a misunderstanding of what it means to follow Him. Jesus is not simply someone we turn to when we need assistance. He is the One to whom we owe our allegiance, and His teaching must shape the way we understand obedience.

In John 14:15, Jesus connects love for Him with obedience to His commandments. This connection challenges the assumption that spiritual devotion can be measured only by emotion, religious activity, or the language we use when speaking about Him. Love for Jesus is not demonstrated perfectly by a person who never struggles, makes mistakes, or needs correction, but it must produce a sincere willingness to follow His teaching and return to His ways when we recognize that we have wandered from them. Obedience is not an attempt to purchase God’s love. It is a response to the One we love and the One whose authority we recognize.

This distinction matters because some people approach obedience as though it were merely a burden imposed upon them, while others use religious activity to create the appearance of devotion without allowing their conduct to change. The Word of God calls us beyond both conditions. We are invited into a relationship with Jesus Christ in which His truth becomes the foundation of our decisions and His example influences how we treat others. We cannot separate our devotion to Him from our responsibility to live according to what He has taught.

A person who genuinely desires to follow Jesus must be willing to examine whether their daily choices reflect His lordship. This examination includes the way we speak when we are angry, the way we respond when someone challenges us, the way we treat those who cannot benefit us, and the way we handle responsibility when we possess an advantage over someone else. It also includes the way we respond when obedience requires us to admit a mistake or surrender the satisfaction of proving ourselves right. The authority of Jesus must reach beyond our public confession and become evident in the private decisions that reveal the condition of our hearts.

Authority Is Not the Same as Control

One of the most important distinctions we must establish is the difference between the authority of God and the authority entrusted to human beings. God possesses supreme authority, while human authority operates within responsibilities and limits. A parent may be responsible for guiding a child, a pastor may be entrusted with shepherding a congregation, an employer may direct work within an organization, and a teacher may carry responsibility for the learning environment. These positions involve legitimate responsibilities, but none of them gives a human being unlimited authority over another person’s conscience or permission to disregard God’s Word.

The existence of authority does not automatically guarantee that it will be exercised correctly. People can make decisions from a place of wisdom and humility, but they can also allow fear, pride, insecurity, or personal ambition to influence the way they lead. A person may possess a genuine responsibility while still needing correction in the way that responsibility is exercised. Recognizing this reality does not require us to reject authority altogether. Instead, it calls us to understand that authority must be exercised according to the principles of righteousness and that those entrusted with responsibility remain accountable for their conduct.

Romans 13:1 to 7 addresses governing authority and the responsibilities associated with it. This passage deserves careful consideration within the broader teaching of Scripture rather than being used as an isolated statement to settle every question concerning human leadership. The Bible also records circumstances in which obedience to God required His servants to refuse demands made by people in positions of power. Acts 5:29 provides a clear example when the apostles declared their obligation to obey God rather than men. Their response establishes an essential boundary: human authority cannot rightfully demand that a believer disobey God.

This principle must be applied with discernment and humility. We should not use our personal preferences as an excuse to label every difficult instruction as contrary to God’s will, nor should we assume that a person’s position makes every demand they make morally or spiritually correct. The question must be examined in light of Scripture, the circumstances involved, and the actual nature of the instruction being given. Genuine discernment requires more than a strong feeling that we are right. It requires a willingness to search the Word, consider the truth honestly, and accept correction when our own interpretation proves mistaken.

Spiritual authority becomes dangerous when a person begins to treat their position as though it gives them the right to control every aspect of another person’s life. Leadership is not ownership, and influence is not permission to manipulate. A pastor cannot replace Jesus Christ as Lord of the believer’s conscience, and a ministry title does not make personal preferences equivalent to divine commandments. Those who lead must remember that their responsibility is to serve faithfully, teach truthfully, and care for the people entrusted to them. Those who follow must learn to recognize and honor godly leadership without abandoning their responsibility to obey God above all.

When Position Is Mistaken for Spiritual Authority

Throughout Scripture, we find examples of people who possessed authority yet failed to exercise it according to God’s will. These examples remind us that position and character are not the same thing, and that receiving responsibility does not remove the need for humility. The presence of a title cannot guarantee that a person’s decisions are wise, just as the absence of a title does not prevent someone from speaking truthfully. God remains the standard by which every person must be evaluated.

King Saul provides a sobering example of what can happen when a person allows personal judgment and the desire to preserve their position to take precedence over obedience to God. In First Samuel 15, Saul received an instruction but did not carry it out as commanded. When confronted, he attempted to defend his actions and explain them in a way that presented his decision as acceptable. His response reveals the danger of substituting our own reasoning for obedience while attempting to maintain the appearance of faithfulness.

The lesson is not that human beings must never ask questions or seek understanding. Rather, it is that we must not use our explanations to excuse deliberate disobedience when God’s instruction is clear. Saul’s position as king did not exempt him from God’s authority, and his desire to preserve his reputation could not change the nature of his responsibility. The same principle applies to every believer and every leader today. We may have sincere reasons for making a decision, but sincerity alone does not make the decision right. When the truth exposes an error, the appropriate response is repentance rather than the construction of excuses designed to protect our image.

This example should lead us to examine our own responses when we are confronted with a mistake. Do we listen carefully and consider whether the correction is justified, or do we immediately search for reasons to defend ourselves? Are we more concerned with understanding what is right than with preserving the impression that we are right? Do we allow Scripture to challenge our decisions, or do we search for an interpretation that will support what we have already chosen to do? These questions matter because the heart that continually protects itself from correction can become increasingly resistant to the very truth it needs.

The same warning applies to spiritual leadership. A leader who refuses correction because of their position has misunderstood the nature of authority. A pastor, teacher, or ministry leader may possess experience and knowledge, but neither experience nor knowledge makes a person incapable of error. Genuine spiritual maturity is demonstrated when leaders remain accountable, acknowledge mistakes honestly, and seek to correct wrongdoing rather than conceal it. The willingness to receive correction does not weaken legitimate authority. It demonstrates a recognition that the leader remains a servant of God rather than the owner of the position they occupy.

The Authority of God’s Word

If we are going to understand authority correctly, we must establish that Scripture is not merely a collection of religious sayings from which we select the passages that support our opinions. The Word of God must be approached with reverence, interpreted in context, and applied with honesty. We cannot claim to submit to God’s authority while deliberately ignoring the parts of His Word that challenge our preferences. Nor can we faithfully teach others if we use isolated passages to demand conduct that the broader message of Scripture does not support.

Hebrews 4:12 to 13 describes the searching power of the Word of God and reminds us that nothing is hidden from His sight. This truth should affect the way we approach biblical study. We do not come to Scripture merely to gather arguments for our existing position. We come to learn, to understand, and to allow God’s truth to expose what requires correction in our thinking and conduct. The same passage that challenges another person’s behavior may also confront something within us, and we must remain willing to receive that examination rather than assuming that its message is intended exclusively for someone else.

This is especially important when discussing submission and spiritual leadership because these subjects can be misused when Scripture is separated from its context. A leader may emphasize the responsibility of believers to respect authority while neglecting the responsibility of leaders to serve faithfully. A believer may emphasize the failures of leaders while neglecting the biblical call to humility, forgiveness, and a teachable spirit. In either case, selecting only the portions of Scripture that support our preferred position can distort the truth we claim to defend.

The proper response is to consider the whole counsel of Scripture, allowing its different teachings to inform our understanding. We must examine what a passage meant within its original context, how it relates to other biblical principles, and how its truth should be applied faithfully today. This process requires patience and humility, especially when the subject involves experiences that have caused us pain or convictions that we have held for many years. We must not allow personal experiences to become the final authority over Scripture, but neither should we ignore genuine wrongdoing simply because someone has attached religious language to it.

The authority of God’s Word is not established by the confidence of the person who quotes it. It is not made stronger by a preacher’s title or weakened by the objections of someone who dislikes what it teaches. Our responsibility is to handle it faithfully, refusing to twist its meaning for personal advantage or use it to place ourselves beyond accountability. Every believer needs this commitment, and every person who teaches the Word carries an especially serious responsibility to represent its message accurately.

The Test of the Heart

The question of authority eventually leads us to the condition of our own hearts, because it is possible to understand many biblical principles intellectually while resisting their application personally. We may recognize the importance of obedience when discussing another person’s decisions but become defensive when the same principle challenges us. We may expect leaders to acknowledge their mistakes while struggling to admit our own. We may demand that others respect our boundaries while refusing to respect theirs. These inconsistencies reveal why spiritual growth requires more than an increase in knowledge. It requires an honest willingness to allow God’s truth to reshape our character.

There are moments when the greatest test of our faith is not whether we can identify what someone else has done wrong, but whether we can respond righteously when we have been treated unfairly. There are moments when we must distinguish between the pain we have experienced and the choices we make in response to that pain. We may have legitimate reasons to question a decision, seek accountability, or establish boundaries, yet we must still guard against allowing bitterness to become the force that governs our lives. Our responsibility is to pursue truth without surrendering our hearts to hatred, to seek justice without becoming consumed by revenge, and to remain faithful to God even when another person’s conduct has disappointed us.

The believer must also recognize that submission to God does not require pretending that every human authority is trustworthy. It does not mean accepting manipulation, concealing wrongdoing, or remaining silent when someone is being harmed. The authority of God establishes a standard that applies to everyone, including those who lead. When a leader acts contrary to Scripture, the situation must be approached with truth, wisdom, and appropriate accountability. Honoring spiritual leadership does not mean declaring every decision correct, and raising a legitimate concern does not automatically mean a person has become rebellious.

At the same time, we must be careful not to use the language of discernment to protect ourselves from every form of correction. Sometimes our resistance comes from genuine concern about what is being asked of us, but sometimes it comes from pride, disappointment, or the simple desire to have our own way. Spiritual maturity requires us to examine these possibilities honestly rather than assuming that our motives are always pure. We must be willing to ask God to reveal where we have misunderstood His Word, where we have responded poorly, and where we need to change.

The goal is not to become people who are easily controlled by others or people who refuse to listen to anyone. The goal is to become people whose hearts are increasingly governed by the truth of God. Such people can receive instruction without losing discernment, ask difficult questions without pursuing conflict, and acknowledge wrongdoing without allowing shame to prevent repentance. They can respect authority while recognizing its limits, and they can exercise leadership without forgetting that their responsibility is ultimately before God.

Living Under the Authority of God

Understanding God’s authority must lead to a practical examination of the way we live. It is not enough to agree that God is sovereign if we continue making decisions without considering His Word. It is not enough to speak about submission if we refuse to surrender the attitudes that prevent us from walking in obedience. It is not enough to teach others about accountability if we become defensive whenever our own conduct is examined. The truth must move from our understanding into our daily choices, influencing the way we speak, the way we lead, the way we respond to correction, and the way we treat those whose circumstances differ from our own.

For the believer, living under God’s authority means developing a heart that is willing to hear His Word and respond faithfully. It means recognizing that personal feelings, cultural expectations, and the opinions of other people cannot replace the truth God has revealed. It means seeking wisdom before making important decisions, examining our motives when conflict arises, and remaining willing to repent when we recognize that we have acted contrary to His principles. Obedience will not always be easy, but it must remain an essential part of our commitment to follow Jesus Christ.

For the leader, living under God’s authority means remembering that responsibility is a trust rather than a personal possession. It means leading with humility, refusing to manipulate others, and recognizing that the people entrusted to our care deserve honesty, compassion, and faithful instruction. It means receiving legitimate questions without automatically interpreting them as attacks, correcting our mistakes when they become apparent, and refusing to use spiritual language to excuse conduct that does not reflect the character of Christ. The greater the influence entrusted to a person, the greater the importance of exercising that influence with integrity.

For the Church, living under God’s authority means creating an environment in which truth is honored, correction is possible, and no person is treated as though their position places them beyond accountability. It means encouraging believers to grow in spiritual maturity rather than cultivating dependence upon human personalities. It means recognizing that unity cannot be built upon the concealment of wrongdoing and that genuine peace requires more than silence. The Church must remain committed to the truth of Scripture while demonstrating the humility and love that should characterize those who follow Jesus Christ.

Ultimately, every one of us must answer the same foundational question: does the authority of God govern the way we live, or have we placed something else in that position? Our answer will be revealed through our conduct, especially when obedience becomes difficult, correction challenges our pride, or responsibility requires us to make a choice that costs us something. We cannot control every circumstance or guarantee that every person will exercise authority wisely, but we can choose to remain faithful to God and to bring our own conduct under the examination of His Word.

The authority behind everything is not the authority of a title, an institution, a tradition, or a human personality. It is the authority of God, whose truth remains the standard by which our lives must be measured. When we understand this, we begin to recognize that submission is not ultimately about elevating another person, and accountability is not merely a means of correcting someone else. Both must be understood in light of our responsibility before the Lord. The believer must learn to obey Him faithfully, and the leader must learn to serve Him faithfully, because neither calling can be separated from the One who holds supreme authority.

This is where our journey beyond the veil truly begins. We must first recognize who God is before we can understand what it means to live under His authority. We must allow His Word to establish our convictions, examine our motives, and direct our conduct before we presume to determine whether someone else is living according to the same standard. As we learn to place every part of our lives beneath the truth of Scripture, we begin to understand that genuine submission is not merely an outward response to instruction. It is the willingness to let God remain God in every area of our lives, even when His truth challenges what we would have chosen for ourselves.

Personal Reflection and Application

Take time to examine your own understanding of God’s authority. Consider whether there are areas of your life where you acknowledge His lordship in words but continue to make decisions according to your own preferences. Reflect upon the way you respond when someone corrects you, when a biblical principle challenges an established belief, or when you are asked to reconsider a decision you have already defended. Be honest about whether pride, fear, disappointment, or a desire for control has influenced your responses.

If you are entrusted with leadership, examine how you exercise that responsibility. Consider whether you welcome legitimate accountability, whether your decisions reflect the principles of Scripture, and whether your influence helps others grow in their relationship with Jesus Christ. If you have experienced the misuse of authority, acknowledge the reality of that experience without allowing it to determine your entire understanding of leadership. Seek wisdom from Scripture, pursue appropriate support and accountability, and remain committed to responding in a manner that honors God.

The purpose of this reflection is not to create condemnation, but to encourage genuine spiritual growth. Godly conviction should lead us toward repentance, understanding, and a more faithful walk with the Lord. Allow the truth you have considered in this chapter to become an opportunity to make meaningful changes in the way you live.

Study Questions

  1. What does it mean to recognize God as the highest authority in every area of life?
  2. How can a person acknowledge Jesus Christ as Lord while continuing to resist His instructions?
  3. What is the difference between God’s supreme authority and the authority entrusted to human beings?
  4. How do the examples of Saul in First Samuel 15 and the apostles in Acts 5:29 help us understand obedience and the limits of human authority?
  5. Why is it important to interpret Scripture in context rather than using individual passages to defend our existing opinions?
  6. How can believers honor spiritual leaders while maintaining their responsibility to test teaching against the Word of God?
  7. What responsibilities accompany spiritual leadership, and why must leaders remain accountable for the way they exercise authority?
  8. What practical changes can you make this week to bring your attitudes, decisions, and relationships into greater alignment with God’s Word?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, I come before You recognizing that You are the highest authority in my life and that Your Word must remain the foundation of my beliefs, decisions, and conduct. Search my heart and reveal the places where I have allowed pride, fear, resentment, or personal ambition to influence my understanding of obedience. Forgive me for the times I have defended my own desires rather than seeking Your will, and give me the humility to receive correction when I need to change.

Teach me to understand authority according to Your Word rather than through assumptions, traditions, or the actions of imperfect people. Help me to honor those who faithfully serve while maintaining the discernment necessary to recognize when something does not agree with Scripture. Protect my heart from bitterness when I experience disappointment, and give me the courage to pursue truth and accountability when wrongdoing must be addressed. Help me to respond with wisdom rather than revenge, with conviction rather than pride, and with a sincere desire to honor You in every circumstance.

If I am entrusted with responsibility over others, teach me to lead with humility, compassion, and integrity. Keep me from using influence to control people or protect my own reputation, and help me remember that every responsibility I carry is ultimately before You. Make me willing to receive correction, acknowledge my mistakes, and pursue what is right even when doing so is difficult.

Let Your Word examine my heart and guide my steps. Help me to become a believer who does not merely speak about obedience but lives it sincerely, and help me to recognize that genuine spiritual maturity is demonstrated through faithfulness to You. May my life reflect the lordship of Jesus Christ in my private decisions, my relationships, my service, and my response to every challenge I face. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

Affirmation of Faith

I acknowledge Jesus Christ as Lord over my life, and I choose to bring my beliefs, decisions, attitudes, and conduct into alignment with His Word. I will seek truth with humility, receive correction with a teachable heart, and exercise discernment without allowing pride or bitterness to govern my responses. I will honor godly leadership without placing any human being above the authority of God, and I will remember that every position of responsibility carries an obligation to serve faithfully. My desire is to walk in obedience, grow in spiritual maturity, and remain accountable to the One who knows my heart. I will not measure faithfulness by outward appearances alone, but by my willingness to follow Jesus Christ and allow His truth to shape the way I live.

Looking Ahead

Understanding the authority of God establishes the foundation, but understanding that foundation is only the beginning. We must also examine what happens within us when we are called to surrender our preferences, receive instruction, or obey when the decision is not our own. In the next chapter, we will explore The Heart of Submission and consider why genuine submission begins within us long before it becomes visible through our actions.
