What Lies Beyond the Veil

By Bradley Hobbs

What Lies Beyond the Veil

There are truths in the Word of God that we can read many times without fully understanding how deeply they are meant to shape our lives. We can become familiar with the language of obedience, submission, authority, and spiritual maturity while never allowing these principles to reach the places within our hearts where they are needed most. We may know what Scripture teaches, recognize its importance, and even encourage others to follow it, yet still struggle when the Word of God challenges our own desires, exposes our attitudes, or calls us to surrender something we have been determined to keep. The greatest challenge is not always discovering what God has said, but allowing His Word to confront what we have convinced ourselves is right.

Authority is one of those subjects that reaches far beyond outward behavior. It touches our relationship with God, our response to correction, our willingness to obey when obedience becomes uncomfortable, and our understanding of the responsibility entrusted to those who lead. It reveals what happens within us when our preferences conflict with God’s instructions, when someone challenges our decisions, or when a person we respect disappoints us through their actions. Our response to these moments can reveal whether our hearts are genuinely surrendered to the Lord or whether we have quietly allowed pride, fear, resentment, or personal ambition to influence our understanding of what it means to walk in obedience.

This is why the subject of authority deserves more than a casual reading of a few familiar passages. It requires us to search the Scriptures with humility, examine the principles God has established, and consider how those principles apply to both the believer who receives instruction and the leader who carries responsibility for others. It requires us to move beyond assumptions and traditions that may have shaped our thinking without ever being carefully tested against the Word of God. Above all, it requires us to recognize that spiritual understanding is not measured merely by how much we know, how confidently we speak, or how long we have occupied a particular position. Genuine spiritual maturity is demonstrated when the truth of God is permitted to shape our character, direct our decisions, and govern our conduct.

The title Beyond the Veil reflects this journey into a deeper understanding of the principles that govern our spiritual lives. A veil can conceal what lies beyond our immediate view, leaving us aware that something exists without fully understanding what it means. In a similar way, our assumptions, experiences, fears, traditions, and personal interpretations can prevent us from seeing the full significance of biblical teaching. We may believe that we understand submission because we have learned to follow instructions, yet never consider the difference between outward compliance and a heart that has genuinely surrendered to God. We may believe that we understand spiritual authority because we have been given a position of responsibility, yet fail to recognize that the authority entrusted to us carries obligations that we cannot dismiss simply because we hold a title. Looking beyond the veil means being willing to examine what lies beneath our outward beliefs and asking whether our understanding is truly rooted in Scripture.

Every discussion about authority must begin with God Himself, because no human being possesses a position that places them above Him. God is the ultimate authority, and His Word provides the standard by which our beliefs, attitudes, decisions, and actions must be examined. Human authority has a place within the order of life, and Scripture addresses the responsibilities of those who govern, teach, shepherd, parent, and lead. However, the existence of human authority does not make every human decision correct, nor does a position of leadership give anyone permission to disregard the principles of righteousness established by God. A person may hold a respected position, possess considerable influence, and speak with great confidence while still needing correction. The same is true of every believer, regardless of experience, reputation, or spiritual standing.

Romans 13:1 to 7 provides an important foundation for understanding governing authority and the responsibilities associated with it. Hebrews 13:17 addresses the relationship between believers and their spiritual leaders while drawing attention to the serious responsibility those leaders carry before God. First Peter 5:2 to 3 further establishes that spiritual leadership must be exercised with a willing heart and a spirit of service rather than through domination or the misuse of position. These passages must be understood together with the wider testimony of Scripture, because God’s Word does not teach us to replace discernment with blind allegiance or to treat human leadership as though it were beyond examination. Acts 5:29 demonstrates that when human demands conflict with God’s commands, obedience to God must remain our highest priority.

Understanding this distinction is essential because submission and accountability are not enemies of one another. A believer can honor spiritual leadership while recognizing that every teaching must remain consistent with Scripture. A leader can exercise genuine authority while remaining humble enough to receive correction and acknowledge mistakes. A person can disagree with a decision without becoming rebellious, just as a person can obey an instruction outwardly while harboring a heart filled with resentment. The outward appearance of a response does not always reveal the condition of the heart, which is why we must learn to examine not only what we do but also why we do it.

The responsibility of the believer is serious because God calls His people to humility, obedience, integrity, and a willingness to receive instruction. We cannot claim to desire the will of God while refusing to examine the places where our own preferences have become more important than His Word. There are times when obedience requires us to admit that we were wrong, release our need to control an outcome, or receive correction that exposes an attitude we would rather defend. There are also times when we must remain patient in circumstances we do not understand, trusting God without allowing frustration to determine our behavior. These moments test whether our faith is merely something we profess or a conviction that genuinely governs our lives.

The responsibility of the leader is equally serious because influence carries obligations that cannot be separated from character. Jesus Christ established a pattern of leadership that stands in contrast to the desire for dominance, personal importance, and control. In Mark 10:42 to 45, He taught His disciples to understand greatness through the spirit of service rather than the exercise of power for personal advantage. This principle challenges every person entrusted with spiritual responsibility to consider whether their leadership draws people toward Jesus Christ or creates an unhealthy dependence upon themselves. A leader is not called to use Scripture to protect a reputation, silence legitimate concerns, or demand unquestioning loyalty. A leader is called to handle the truth faithfully, care for those entrusted to them, and remember that the people they serve belong to God.

This does not mean that every disagreement represents spiritual abuse or that every difficult instruction should be rejected. Faithful leadership sometimes requires difficult conversations, necessary correction, and decisions that may not please everyone. Likewise, spiritual maturity sometimes requires believers to receive instruction that challenges their preferences or exposes their mistakes. The difference lies in whether authority is exercised according to God’s principles and whether the people involved remain willing to pursue truth, righteousness, and accountability. Correction that seeks restoration is different from humiliation intended to establish control, and biblical submission is different from surrendering one’s conscience to another person. Learning to recognize these distinctions requires careful study, discernment, prayer, and a sincere commitment to follow the truth wherever it leads.

We must also understand that the failures of others do not remove our own responsibility before God. When a leader treats someone unfairly, misuses Scripture, or refuses to acknowledge wrongdoing, the injured person may experience genuine pain and confusion. Such experiences should never be minimized, and forgiveness must never be used as an excuse to conceal wrongdoing or prevent appropriate accountability. At the same time, we must guard our hearts against allowing another person’s actions to become the foundation of our own conduct. We can acknowledge wrongdoing without surrendering ourselves to bitterness, pursue accountability without seeking revenge, and establish necessary boundaries without abandoning our commitment to live according to the Word of God. Our responsibility is not to pretend that injustice has not occurred, but to respond in a manner that remains faithful to the Lord.

Throughout this book, we will examine the principles that shape our understanding of authority and the ways those principles influence our relationships with God and with one another. We will consider the condition of the heart, the meaning of submission, the demands of obedience, the responsibility of spiritual leadership, and the dangers that arise when pride, offense, fear, or ambition begin to govern our responses. We will also explore the importance of correction, repentance, discernment, and accountability, recognizing that these principles are not reserved for a particular group within the Church. They apply to every person who claims to follow Jesus Christ, whether that person is receiving instruction, teaching others, or carrying responsibility for a congregation or ministry.

The purpose of this journey is not to encourage suspicion toward every leader or to create an atmosphere in which every instruction is treated as a threat. Neither is it to protect people in positions of authority from legitimate questions or the consequences of their actions. The purpose is to bring our understanding back to the Word of God and allow its truth to challenge every assumption that cannot withstand examination. We must be willing to ask difficult questions about our own motives before we presume to judge the motives of others, and we must also be willing to confront wrongdoing when faithfulness to God, the protection of others, and the pursuit of righteousness require it.

As we move through these teachings, the believer must be willing to ask whether their heart remains teachable when correction becomes uncomfortable, whether they honor others without placing them above the Word of God, and whether they can respond to disappointment without allowing resentment to direct their decisions. The leader must be willing to ask whether their influence is being used to serve or control, whether their teaching reflects the truth of Scripture, and whether they are prepared to accept accountability when their decisions or conduct require correction. These questions are not intended to condemn us without hope, but to bring us into a more honest understanding of our responsibilities and a deeper willingness to allow God to change us.

There is a difference between knowing the language of submission and living with a surrendered heart. There is a difference between occupying a position of authority and faithfully carrying the responsibility that comes with it. There is also a difference between defending our own understanding and humbly allowing the Word of God to correct it. These differences may not always be visible to those around us, but they matter deeply because our relationship with God cannot be reduced to outward appearances. He calls us to a life in which our convictions, attitudes, words, and actions are increasingly shaped by His truth.

My prayer is that Beyond the Veil will encourage every reader to approach these teachings with an open heart, a discerning mind, and a sincere desire to grow in the knowledge of God. May the believer learn to walk in obedience without surrendering biblical discernment, and may the leader learn to exercise authority with humility, integrity, and a genuine spirit of service. May correction lead to repentance rather than defensiveness, may truth be spoken with courage and wisdom, and may accountability become an expression of faithfulness rather than something to be feared. Above all, may we become more concerned with pleasing Jesus Christ than with protecting our pride, defending our reputation, or maintaining control over the opinions of others.

The veil must first be lifted from our own hearts. Before we ask whether another person understands authority, we must examine whether we understand the authority of God. Before we insist that others receive correction, we must be willing to receive it ourselves. Before we demand that our position be respected, we must consider whether our conduct reflects the character of the One we claim to serve. This is where genuine spiritual examination begins, because the truth we discover beyond the veil must ultimately become the truth by which we are willing to live.

The question before us is not simply whether we believe in authority, whether we value obedience, or whether we understand the responsibilities of leadership. The deeper question is whether we are willing to allow the Word of God to search our hearts, challenge our assumptions, expose our errors, and lead us into greater faithfulness. As we begin this journey, may we approach every chapter with the humility to learn, the courage to confront what must change, and the faith to trust that obedience to God is never an invitation to place our confidence in human perfection. It is a call to live under the lordship of Jesus Christ, whose truth must remain our foundation and whose authority must govern every part of our lives.

Our journey begins with the most important question of all: who holds the highest authority, and what does it truly mean to live as though we believe the answer?
